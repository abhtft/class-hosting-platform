# Clean portfolio page (a recipe)

Use this when the student wants a personal page: who they are, what they made, how to reach them. It should be fast to read and easy to keep up to date.

## Sections, in this order
1. **Header:** the name, one line about what they do or study, and a short navigation (About, Projects, Contact). Keep it to three or four items.
2. **Hero:** a heading with the name or the main claim, one or two sentences, and one main button (for example "See my projects"). A photo is optional; if used, it needs alt text.
3. **About:** two short paragraphs at most. What they care about, what they are learning, what they enjoy building.
4. **Projects:** a grid of three to six cards. Each card has an image or colour block, a title, one sentence, and a link or button. Put the best project first.
5. **Skills or interests:** a short list of words, not progress bars. Progress bars say nothing.
6. **Contact:** a way to reach them (an email link or a short form, see the forms skill) and links to profiles they choose to share.
7. **Footer:** the year and the name. Nothing else is needed.

## Content rules
- Write in the first person, in short plain sentences. Say what you did and what happened, not only the topic.
- Every project card answers: what is it, what did I do, what did I learn. Link to the live page if there is one.
- Use real work. If there are only two projects, show two. Do not add placeholders that look finished.
- Do not publish a phone number, home address or other private details unless the student decides to.

## Look and feel
- Follow the frontend design and responsive skills: one font, one accent colour, generous spacing, a centred column about 1000px wide at most.
- Use a single page with anchor links, or separate routes only if there is a real reason.
- Keep images small (resize before adding them; aim for under 200 KB each). Use modern formats if the student's tools allow it.
- Make the page work well on a phone first, then widen it.

## Finishing
1. Read it as a stranger: in ten seconds, can you tell who this is and what they do?
2. Check the accessibility list: headings in order, alt text, contrast, keyboard use.
3. Check every link and button works, then deploy.
